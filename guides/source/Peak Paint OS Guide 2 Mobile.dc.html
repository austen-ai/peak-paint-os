01 · THE DAY, START TO FINISHP H O N E

Four taps a day.

Clock on when you get there. Sign on to the safety sheet. Mark off what you painted before you leave. Clock off.

01Clock on and pick today's job
02Sign on to the SWMS
03Mark off progress and take photos
04Flag extra work as a variation
05Request paint and materials
06Quick quote for small work, from site

Your call, Danny: will the crew clock on in the app, or do you want it to happen automatically when they arrive on site?

NO JOB TODAYP H O N E

Hours that are not on a job.

Travel, training, yard time, rained off. The clock still runs — it just goes to an indirect bucket instead of a job, so the hours are paid but not charged to a client.

ALWAYS AT THE BOTTOM OF MY JOBS

Real jobs first, then the five indirect buckets. They never disappear, so there is always something to clock on to.

THE FIVE BUCKETS
Travel
IND-TRAVEL
Between sites, or to the yard
Training
IND-TRAINING
Courses, inductions, toolbox time
Yard / workshop
IND-YARD
Loading out, gear, cleaning up
Inclement weather
IND-WEATHER
Rained off, too windy to spray
Other
IND-OTHER
Anything that fits nowhere else
HOW IT RUNS
1Open Jobs — real jobs sit above the indirect buckets
2Tap the bucket that matches, same as tapping a job
3Clock on. The timer runs exactly as it does on a job
4Hours land on the timesheet marked non-chargeable

Your call, Danny: are these the right five, and should travel between two jobs in one day be charged to a job instead of sitting here?

02 · THE JOB SCREENP H O N E

Everything for this job, one tap away.

Open the job and there are five things: progress, photos, variations, the safety sheet, and materials. Nothing else to learn.

WHAT EACH ONE DOES
ProgressSlide each area to what is actually done
PhotosBefore, during, after — tagged to the room
VariationsExtra work found on site, with photos
SWMSTick the controls, sign on
MaterialsPaint and gear needed, charged to this job

Your call: is that the right five, and which one should be the first thing they see?

03 · SIGN ON TO THE SWMSP H O N E

No sign-on, no start.

The safety sheet you wrote at HQ shows up here. The painter ticks the controls that are actually in place, then signs. It is logged against the job with the time.

WHAT THE OFFICE GETS

A line in the sign-on log: name, job, time. That is the record that the crew read the sheet before they started.

If a control is not in place, they leave it unticked — and it should be a conversation, not a tick.

Your call: should the app block progress capture until the SWMS is signed, or just flag it to you?

04 · MARK OFF PROGRESSP H O N E

This is the number you invoice from.

One card per area. Slide it to what is actually done, tick the surfaces finished, add a photo. Then Submit update.

HOW IT BECOMES 53%

Each area carries its share of the contract value, so the job percentage is a value-weighted average — a big elevation moves it more than a cupboard.

Nobody re-estimates it at the office. What the crew slide is what the client is billed on.

Best done at the end of each day, on site, while it is in front of you.

Your call: are areas and surfaces the right way to break a job up, or would the crew rather tick off whole rooms?

05 · FLAG EXTRA WORKP H O N E

Never start extra work on a nod.

Rot, water damage, a wall that needs a gib repair — photograph it, pick the rate, enter the quantity, submit. It is priced and waiting for you at HQ.

WHAT HAPPENS NEXT
1It lands on the HQ dashboard as to price
2You check the photos, then approve or decline
3Approved ones go on the next progress report with the photos
4The value is added to the contract sum

Your call: should the crew see the value at all, or just describe the work and photograph it?

06 · MATERIALS & QUICK QUOTEP H O N E

Paint ordered, small jobs priced.

Two smaller things the app does from site — a materials request tied to the job, and a quote for work you can hold in your head.

Your call: should a quick quote be sendable to the client on the spot, or always come back to you first?

FEEDBACKP H O N E

What to tell us.

Try it on a real day with a real painter. Anything that takes more than a few seconds on site will not get done — that is the feedback worth having.
